How Stagger works

Stagger is a range agent and LP ladder manager for Uniswap v3 on Robinhood Chain. The agent proposes; you sign. Your ladder stays yours, and every number is shown with its source.

Use it in one minute

  1. Open the agent. The top pool by 24h volume is already loaded; pick another from the list or the pools table.
  2. Choose a horizon (1, 3, 7, 14 or 30 days) and a side: two-sided, below the price (buy as it falls) or above the price (sell as it rises).
  3. Enter the most you want to put in of each token. Two-sided needs both tokens; one-sided needs only one.
  4. Read the plan: range, shape, bins, chance of staying inside, fee estimate, and every reason the agent gives.
  5. Press Sign & open ladder. Your wallet asks for a token approval if needed, then one transaction opens all bins.
  6. Manage it under Portfolio: claim fees, withdraw selected bins, or close.

How the agent decides

Measure. It reads the pool live (tick, liquidity, fee tier) and up to 168 hourly prices: GeckoTerminal hourly candles when they match the live pool price, otherwise the pool's own on-chain oracle (TWAP). From the hourly returns it computes daily volatility (σ), autocorrelation (does the price snap back?) and a trend score (drift relative to noise).

Shape.

  • Bid-Ask when σ is above 5% a day or the price trends strongly: more liquidity toward the edges, where it is paid as the price travels. Default 12 bins, z = 1.64.
  • Curve when σ is below 1.5% a day and the price tends to snap back: most liquidity near the current price. Default 9 bins, z = 1.0.
  • Spot otherwise: liquidity spread evenly. Default 1 bin, z = 1.28.
  • No usable history: the agent assumes 5% a day and goes wide.

Range. Half-width = z × σ_day × √days, aligned to the pool's tick spacing. One-sided ranges sit fully below or above the price with twice that width. For two-sided ranges the plan shows the chance the price is still inside after the horizon in a normal model, 2Φ(z) − 1.

Bins. The range is cut into 1–40 bins; each bin is one Uniswap v3 position, so each one costs gas to open. Your amounts are scaled so neither token goes over what you entered.

Fees. The estimate is your share of the pool's in-range liquidity times the last 24h volume times the fee tier. It is zero while the price is outside your range.

Track record. Every 6 hours the agent publishes a 1-day call for the top pools and later records whether the price stayed inside. The scoreboard shows all of them.

What the contracts can and cannot do

LadderManager opens 1–40 Uniswap v3 positions in one transaction and holds them for you.

  • Only the ladder's owner can claim, withdraw selected bins, or close it. Funds go back to the owner only.
  • The protocol fee is 5% of the ETH side of claimed swap fees, set at deploy, capped at 10% in code, and never taken from principal. Fees in USDG or any other token go 100% to the owner.
  • No admin, no owner key, no proxy, no upgrade, no pause. Nobody can move your positions.
  • Every bin has a slippage floor and the open has a deadline.

StaggerStake only receives ETH (the fee cut) and streams it to $RUNGS stakers over 7 days. It swaps nothing.

  • Stake, unstake and claim at any time; no lock.
  • The $RUNGS address is set once after launch and can never change. There is no other admin function.

The agent and the API never hold keys or funds. They return a plan and the transaction data; your wallet signs it.

Contract addresses appear here once deployed.

Not audited. Both contracts have fork tests against Robinhood Chain mainnet, but no external audit. The agent's maths is a model, not a guarantee. Use amounts you can afford to lose.

Risks

  • Impermanent loss. As the price moves, your ladder converts one token into the other. You can end with less value than if you had held.
  • Out of range earns nothing. A bin only earns fees while the price is inside it. If the price leaves the whole range, the ladder sits idle in one token.
  • Past prices are not future prices. Volatility, the shape choice and the stay probability come from recent history; markets change.
  • Gas. Each bin is a position; more bins cost more gas to open, withdraw and close.
  • Smart-contract risk. Uniswap v3, the tokens in the pool, and Stagger's own unaudited contracts can have bugs.
  • $RUNGS. No promised returns. The ETH stream depends entirely on fees being claimed.

API and MCP for other agents

The same plan is public as JSON and as MCP tools (stagger_plan, stagger_pools).

curl "https://staggeragent.xyz/api/pools" curl "https://staggeragent.xyz/api/plan?pool=0x…&horizonDays=7&mode=both&amount0=1&amount1=2000" # MCP: point the Stagger MCP server at the public API STAGGER_API=https://staggeragent.xyz
